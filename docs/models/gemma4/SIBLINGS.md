## 0. TL;DR

- **mlx-lm (no KV quant) is the Gemma4 decode champion at every size on every
  model** it can run. On the two large models it ties mlx-lm-turboquant within
  noise (decode is weight-bandwidth-bound, KV quant buys nothing); on the small
  models it edges turboquant slightly (no KV-dequant overhead).
- **Stock mlx-lm cannot load Gemma4 e2b / e4b** — a `sanitize()` gap on
  KV-shared layers. The turboquant fork carries the fix; its no-KV-quant mode is
  used as the plain-mlx-lm reference for those two. Stock mlx-lm **does** load
  the kv-shared=0 models (26b, 31b).
- **mlx-lm-turboquant cannot run the 26b MoE** — its fused-MoE Metal kernel
  asserts `K % 512 == 0`; gemma4-26b's intermediate dim 2816 fails it.
- **oMLX trails the mlx-lm tier by 15–40 % on the small models and is
  non-representative on the large ones** — it forces an SSD-paged KV cache for
  26b/31b, collapsing decode to ~0.1–1.4 TPS at 64k/128k (I/O-bound, not
  compute). Its prefill numbers are meaningless (auto-prefix-cache → r0 TTFT
  ~30 ms regardless of context).
- **ollama, mlx-vlm-as-text, paroquant/PARO** are skipped — see §4 for reasons
  (no Gemma4 MLX tag; out-of-scope modality axis; serve-time crash).

## 3. Decode-efficiency analysis

**1. mlx-lm vs turboquant — KV quant buys nothing on Gemma4 decode.** On the two
models turboquant can run (e2b, e4b, 31b), KV 8,4 is within ±2–4 % of plain
mlx-lm and *loses* at short context (KV-dequant overhead > bandwidth saved when
KV is tiny). It only reaches parity / a hair ahead at 64k on e4b. Gemma4 uses
**512/1024-token sliding-window attention on most layers**, so the resident KV
is small relative to the weight stream even at 64k — decode is **weight-bandwidth
bound**, and quantizing KV doesn't move the bottleneck. This is most extreme on
31b: mlx-lm and tq are identical to 3 sig-figs at every size (~14→7.6 TPS),
consistent with the recorded fact that gemma-4-31b decode is dense-mxfp8
bandwidth physics, not a tunable.

**2. stock mlx-lm can't load e2b / e4b (KV-shared sanitize bug).** Stock mlx-lm
`gemma4_text.py::sanitize()` does not drop `k_proj`/`v_proj`/`k_norm` for the
KV-shared layers (e2b: layers 15–34, `num_kv_shared_layers=20`; e4b: 18 shared).
Load aborts with `ValueError: Received 100 parameters not in model`. The
turboquant fork added the drop rule, so its binary loads them — that is why the
"mlx-lm" rows for e2b/e4b are produced by the fork in no-KV-quant mode. Stock
mlx-lm loads 26b/31b fine (`num_kv_shared_layers=0`). *Upstream gap — file
against mlx-lm.*

**3. turboquant fused-MoE kernel rejects gemma4-26b.** First decode token on the
26b MoE crashes: `AssertionError: fused kernel requires K divisible by 512, got
K=2816` (`fused_moe_kernel.py:421`). gemma4-26b's MoE intermediate dim is 2816,
not a multiple of 512. No turboquant data for the 26b MoE; recorded as a
hard skip, not a slow cell.

**4. oMLX — two distinct distortions, both make it non-championable here.**
   - *Small models (e2b/e4b):* loads via its **VLM engine path**, decodes
     coherently, but trails mlx-lm by 15–40 %. The `r1` run is still cold
     (multi-step warmup) so the n=4 median CI is wide at 32k (e.g. e2b 32k
     [43.2–79.4]); the median (~78) is right, the CI is warmup-contaminated.
   - *Large models (26b/31b):* oMLX forces an **SSD-paged KV cache**
     (block_size 1024). At 4k–32k warm runs recover to ~35–49 (26b) / 6–9 (31b)
     TPS, but at 64k/128k decode collapses to **0.1–1.4 TPS** — pure SSD I/O,
     not compute. RSS balloons to 41–61 GB. These cells measure oMLX's tiering
     policy, not the model's decode speed; **excluded from champion designation.**
   - *Prefill:* oMLX auto-prefix-caches; r0 TTFT is ~30–160 ms at every context
     → its prefill TPS is a cache-hit artifact (e.g. "823k tok/s" at 64k), not
     real prefill. Excluded from §2e.

**5. ollama has no Gemma4 MLX build.** `ollama list` is empty of gemma4; the
official Ollama library ships no Gemma4-in-MLX tag (only GGUF, out of scope).
The ~2× decode illusion the Qwen3.6 campaign chased does not even arise here.

## 5. Notes

### Machine and environment
- **Chip:** Apple M5 Max, 128 GB unified memory
- **OS:** macOS 26.4.1 (Darwin 25.4.0)
- **Date:** 2026-06-07
- **Single-MLX discipline:** every backend switch preflighted
  (`pkill mlx_lm/omlx/paroquant/rmlx; rm claim`). One MLX process at a time.
- **No `purge` between cells** — macOS buffer cache may hold recently-read
  safetensors pages. Decode (steady-state) is insensitive; prefill r0 is cold
  per fresh server start.

### Caveats carried into Stage 2 / synthesis
- 64k/128k are **n=1 measured** — bump to n≥2 if a close call decides a champion.
- e2b/e4b **lack a 128k cell** (fixture > ctx) — build a ≤131k-token Gemma
  fixture to close it.
- oMLX large-model rows are **SSD-policy artifacts**, not compute — do not crown
  or compare them.
- The **mlx-lm decode champion is the number rMLX must beat** in Stage 2:
  e2b ~116 / e4b ~75 / 26b ~74 / 31b ~14 TPS @ 4k, scaling down with context as
  tabulated. (rMLX's own perf_canary anchors put Gemma4-e4b at ~74 TPS — at or
  above this mlx-lm reference; Stage 2 confirms per-cell.)
